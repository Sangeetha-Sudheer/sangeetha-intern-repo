# Agile Ceremonies & Team Collaboration

## Main Agile Ceremonies

### Daily Stand-ups

A daily stand-up is a short team meeting used to share progress and identify blockers.

Team members usually discuss:

- what they completed;
- what they are currently working on;
- any blockers or problems;
- anything the team needs to know.

The purpose is to keep everyone aligned without having a long meeting.

## Sprint Planning vs Continuous Prioritisation

### Sprint Planning in Scrum

In Scrum, sprint planning is used to decide what work the team will complete during the next sprint.

The team reviews the backlog, discusses priorities, estimates work, and agrees on a sprint goal.

### Continuous Prioritisation in Kanban

Kanban does not require fixed sprints.

Instead, work is continuously prioritised based on urgency, value, and team capacity.

When one task is completed, another high-priority task can be moved into progress.

This makes Kanban flexible when priorities change frequently.

## Retrospectives

A retrospective gives the team an opportunity to reflect on how they worked together.

The team can discuss:

- what went well;
- what did not go well;
- problems that affected the team;
- improvements to try in the future.

The goal is continuous improvement rather than blaming individuals.

## Backlog Refinement

Backlog refinement is the process of reviewing future tasks before they are started.

The team may:

- clarify requirements;
- break large tasks into smaller tasks;
- change priorities;
- identify dependencies;
- discuss technical questions;
- make sure the task is ready to be worked on.

This makes future development work easier to understand and plan.

## Asynchronous Collaboration

Agile teams do not always need to communicate through meetings.

Teams can collaborate asynchronously using tools such as:

- GitHub issues;
- pull requests;
- task boards;
- Teams or Slack messages;
- documentation;
- written progress updates.

Asynchronous communication is especially useful when team members work at different times or across different time zones.

Clear written updates help other team members understand progress without needing everyone to be online at the same time.

## How Agile Ceremonies Improve Communication and Alignment

Agile ceremonies create regular opportunities for team members to share information.

Stand-ups help the team understand current progress and blockers.

Planning and prioritisation help everyone understand what work is most important.

Retrospectives allow the team to improve its working process.

Backlog refinement helps ensure tasks are clear before development begins.

Together, these activities reduce misunderstandings and help the team stay focused on shared goals.

## Ceremony Most Important for My Role

For my role as a Backend Developer Intern, I find stand-ups particularly useful.

A stand-up gives me a clear opportunity to explain:

- what I completed;
- what I am currently working on;
- whether I am blocked;
- whether I need clarification or support.

This is useful because technical problems can sometimes take longer than expected.

Sharing blockers early gives the team a chance to help before the issue delays the task further.

## Stand-up Observation

I attended a team meeting where progress updates were shared.

During the meeting, team members discussed what they had been working on, current tasks, and any issues or blockers.

I noticed that short updates made it easier to understand what everyone was working on without needing a long discussion.

If a topic needed more discussion, it could be followed up separately after the main updates.

## Retrospective / Review Reflection

I reviewed how the team communicates progress and responds to issues.

One useful part of the process is discussing what worked well and what could be improved.

This encourages the team to learn from previous work instead of repeating the same problems.

For example, clearer task descriptions, earlier communication about blockers, and keeping task boards updated can make collaboration smoother.

## One Change I Can Make to Improve Team Collaboration

One change I can make is to communicate blockers earlier.

If I am stuck on a technical issue, I should avoid spending too long trying to solve it alone without updating the team.

Instead, I can explain:

- what I am trying to do;
- what I have already tried;
- the error or blocker;
- what support I need.

This would make it easier for teammates to help me and reduce unnecessary delays.

## Reflection

Agile ceremonies help teams communicate regularly without relying only on large formal meetings.

For me, they provide structure for sharing progress, asking questions, receiving feedback, and identifying blockers.

I think they are most effective when updates are short, relevant, and followed by clear actions.

Using both meetings and asynchronous communication can help the team stay aligned while still giving developers enough focused time to complete their work.
