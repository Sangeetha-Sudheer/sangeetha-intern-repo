# Agile Principles and Frameworks

## What is Agile?

Agile is an approach to software development that focuses on delivering useful work in small increments, receiving feedback regularly, and adapting when requirements change.

Traditional project management often plans most of the work in advance and follows a more fixed sequence of stages. Agile is more flexible because teams can review progress frequently and adjust priorities when needed.

## Core Agile Values

The Agile Manifesto describes four main values:

1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation.
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
4. Responding to change over following a plan.

These values do not mean that processes, documentation, contracts, or plans are unimportant. They mean that Agile teams give more attention to communication, useful outcomes, collaboration, and adaptability.

## Scrum and Kanban

Scrum and Kanban are both Agile approaches, but they organise work differently.

### Scrum

Scrum usually organises work into fixed periods called sprints.

A Scrum team commonly uses:

- a product backlog;
- sprint planning;
- short sprints;
- daily stand-ups;
- sprint reviews;
- retrospectives;
- defined responsibilities such as Product Owner and Scrum Master.

The team chooses a set of work for a sprint and aims to complete it before the sprint ends.

### Kanban

Kanban focuses on continuous workflow instead of fixed sprints.

Work is normally shown on a visual board using stages such as:

- To Do
- In Progress
- Review
- Done

Kanban also uses work-in-progress limits so that the team does not start too many tasks at the same time.

New work can be added and prioritised as capacity becomes available.

## Main Differences Between Scrum and Kanban

The main difference is that Scrum works in fixed sprints, while Kanban supports a continuous flow of work.

Scrum normally has scheduled planning, reviews, and sprint goals. Kanban is more flexible because tasks can move through the workflow continuously.

Scrum can be useful when a team wants structured iterations and predictable sprint cycles.

Kanban can be useful when priorities change regularly or when a team needs to manage many different tasks continuously.

## Why Focus Bear Uses a Kanban-Style Approach

A Kanban-style approach suits work where tasks can vary in size and priority.

For a development team, this allows work such as bug fixes, feature development, technical improvements, and onboarding tasks to move through the workflow as capacity becomes available.

A visual board also makes it easier for team members to see what is waiting, what is currently being worked on, and what has been completed.

This supports flexibility and makes work progress easier to track.

## Reflection

### What are the biggest benefits and challenges of Agile?

One of the biggest benefits of Agile is flexibility. A team can respond to feedback and changing requirements without waiting until the end of a large project.

Agile also encourages regular communication and collaboration, which can help problems become visible earlier.

Another benefit is that work is completed in smaller pieces, making progress easier to review.

A challenge is that Agile requires good communication and organisation. If priorities change too often or tasks are not clearly defined, team members may become confused about what they should work on.

Agile teams also need to keep their task boards and progress information updated so that everyone has an accurate view of the work.

### How can Agile principles help me in my role?

Agile principles can help me as a backend developer by encouraging me to break larger tasks into smaller pieces and complete them incrementally.

I can regularly test my work, ask for feedback, and make improvements instead of waiting until the entire task is finished.

Using an Agile workflow also helps me communicate my progress clearly and identify blockers early.

It encourages me to adapt when requirements change and focus on delivering useful, working results.

## Agile Principle Most Useful to Me

The Agile principle I find most useful is responding to change.

During development, requirements, priorities, or technical details can change. Being willing to adapt helps me avoid becoming too dependent on one original plan.

Instead, I can review the new information, adjust my approach, and continue working toward the required outcome.

## Summary

Scrum and Kanban are both Agile approaches.

Scrum organises work into fixed sprints with planned activities and defined roles.

Kanban focuses on a continuous flow of work using a visual board and limits on work in progress.

For my work, Kanban is useful because it provides a clear view of tasks while allowing priorities to change when necessary.

Agile overall helps me work in smaller steps, communicate progress, respond to feedback, and continuously improve my work.
