# Roles in an Agile Team

## Key Roles in an Agile Team

### Developers

Developers are responsible for designing, building, testing, fixing, and improving the product.

They turn requirements into working features and collaborate with other team members to make sure the software works correctly.

Developers also participate in planning, reviews, debugging, and continuous improvement.

### QA (Quality Assurance)

QA focuses on checking that the product behaves as expected.

QA responsibilities can include:

- testing features;
- identifying bugs;
- checking edge cases;
- verifying fixes;
- helping define acceptance criteria;
- supporting automated testing.

QA works closely with developers so issues can be found and fixed early.

### Product Manager

The Product Manager helps decide what should be built and why.

They focus on user needs, business goals, priorities, and the overall direction of the product.

They communicate requirements to the team and help prioritise work based on value and impact.

### Agile Project Manager

The Agile Project Manager helps the team stay organised and supports the Agile process.

Their responsibilities may include:

- helping track progress;
- removing blockers;
- supporting communication;
- helping organise planning and review activities;
- making sure work is visible and priorities are clear.

### Other Supporting Roles

Other roles can also contribute to an Agile team.

Examples include:

- stakeholders;
- UX or UI designers;
- customer support;
- security specialists;
- technical leads;
- clients or users.

These people provide feedback, requirements, specialist knowledge, or user perspectives that help the team make better decisions.

## How Roles Collaborate in Scrum and Kanban

In Scrum, collaboration is often organised around fixed sprints.

Developers, QA, Product Managers, and other team members work toward a sprint goal. Work is usually discussed during sprint planning, daily stand-ups, reviews, and retrospectives.

In Kanban, collaboration is more continuous.

Team members move work through stages such as To Do, In Progress, Review, and Done.

Instead of waiting for the next sprint, people can respond to changing priorities as capacity becomes available.

In both approaches, communication between roles is important because developers need clear requirements, QA needs to understand expected behaviour, and Product Managers need feedback about progress and technical constraints.

## My Role in the Agile Team

My role is Backend Developer Intern.

I contribute to the Agile process by working on backend tasks such as:

- API development;
- testing;
- debugging;
- database-related work;
- code quality improvements;
- technical documentation;
- fixing issues identified during review.

I also update my progress, ask questions when requirements are unclear, and provide evidence when tasks are completed.

Working in small steps helps me test changes and get feedback before moving on.

## How My Responsibilities Interact With Other Roles

My work overlaps with several other roles.

I interact with QA because the backend features I develop need to be tested and verified.

I also interact with Product Managers or mentors because they help clarify requirements and priorities.

When a bug is found, I may need to reproduce the issue, identify the cause, fix it, and then work with QA or reviewers to confirm the fix.

This collaboration helps reduce misunderstandings and makes it easier to deliver working features.

## Role I Frequently Collaborate With

One role I frequently collaborate with is QA.

As a developer, I focus on implementing the feature or fixing the backend issue.

QA focuses on checking whether the feature behaves correctly under different conditions.

We can improve teamwork by:

- sharing clear reproduction steps for bugs;
- explaining expected behaviour;
- communicating changes that may affect testing;
- providing test evidence;
- discussing edge cases early;
- confirming when a fix is ready to be retested.

This can reduce repeated work and help issues move through the workflow faster.

## Reflection

### What is my role and how does it contribute to the Agile process?

As a Backend Developer Intern, my role is to build, test, debug, and improve backend functionality.

I contribute to the Agile process by completing tasks in smaller pieces, testing my work, responding to feedback, and communicating progress.

This helps the team continuously deliver improvements instead of waiting until a large amount of work is finished.

### How do my responsibilities overlap or interact with other roles?

My responsibilities interact closely with QA, Product Management, and other developers.

I depend on clear requirements from the Product Manager or mentor, and QA depends on the implementation being testable and understandable.

I also work with other developers when changes affect shared parts of the system.

Because these responsibilities overlap, clear communication is important for avoiding duplicated work and misunderstandings.

## Summary

An Agile team includes different roles that contribute in different ways.

Developers build and improve the product, QA checks quality, Product Managers guide priorities, Agile Project Managers support the workflow, and supporting roles provide additional knowledge and feedback.

My Backend Developer Intern role fits into this process by delivering backend work, testing changes, responding to feedback, and collaborating with others to move tasks toward completion.
