# Inclusive Design Reflection

## Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?

Vulnerable populations can include people who may experience barriers because of disability, neurodivergence, age, health, social circumstances, or limited access to technology.

In the context of Focus Bear, neurodivergent users such as people with ADHD or Autism may experience challenges with attention, executive functioning, sensory overload, motivation, and frustration tolerance.

Digital products can become harder to use when they contain too much information, unclear instructions, unpredictable navigation, excessive notifications, confusing layouts, or too many decisions at once.

## Ethical considerations when working with neurodivergent individuals

Important ethical considerations include treating users with dignity, respecting different communication and sensory needs, avoiding overwhelming experiences, and not making assumptions about what every neurodivergent user needs.

Communication should be clear, patient, respectful, and supportive.

Design should also give users control where possible instead of forcing rigid behaviour.

## Making interactions and content more accessible

Some ways to make digital experiences more accessible include:

- Using simple and clear language.
- Keeping navigation predictable.
- Reducing unnecessary cognitive load.
- Breaking large tasks into smaller steps.
- Avoiding excessive notifications or distracting visual elements.
- Giving clear feedback after user actions.
- Allowing users to customise settings.
- Using consistent terminology throughout the product.

## Supporting neurodivergent team members

In a professional setting, neurodivergent team members can be supported by:

- Communicating expectations clearly.
- Providing written instructions when useful.
- Respecting different working and communication styles.
- Allowing reasonable flexibility in how work is completed.
- Avoiding unnecessary ambiguity.
- Giving constructive feedback in a clear and respectful way.
- Creating an environment where people can ask questions without feeling judged.

## How I can adjust my communication style

I can make my communication more inclusive by being specific, clear, and patient.

Instead of giving vague instructions, I can explain what needs to be done, why it matters, and what the expected outcome is.

I should also avoid assuming that silence means understanding and give people the opportunity to ask questions or request clarification.

## Common UX and communication pitfalls

Some common problems that can make Focus Bear less accessible include:

- Too many options shown at once.
- Long or complicated instructions.
- Unclear error messages.
- Inconsistent navigation.
- Too many notifications.
- Strong sensory distractions.
- Using vague language.
- Making users feel blamed for struggling to complete a task.

These issues can be reduced through clear language, predictable design, smaller steps, supportive feedback, and customisation.

## One practical change I can make

One practical change I can make in my backend and development work is to think about how API errors and system messages will eventually be presented to the user.

Instead of returning unclear or overly technical errors, I should help create responses that allow the frontend to show clear, understandable, and supportive messages.

## First-person perspective learning

A common theme in first-person accounts from people with ADHD and Autism is that productivity tools can become overwhelming when they require too much setup, create too many reminders, or punish users for missing routines.

This highlights the importance of flexibility, reduced cognitive load, clear feedback, and user control.

## Design or communication improvement for Focus Bear

One improvement that could make Focus Bear more accessible is to provide a simplified onboarding mode where users are introduced to one feature at a time instead of being shown many settings at once.

This could reduce cognitive load and make the product easier to understand for users who struggle with executive functioning.

## Example supportive response

If a user says they are struggling to complete a routine, I would respond clearly and supportively:

"Thanks for letting us know. It may help to simplify the routine and start with one or two smaller steps. You can adjust the routine so it better matches what feels manageable for you. If something in the app is making the process confusing, please tell us which part and we can help."

## Reflection

This task helped me understand that accessibility is not only about technical compliance. It is also about reducing unnecessary difficulty and making communication predictable, respectful, and supportive.

As a developer, I should consider how backend decisions, error handling, data structures, and application behaviour affect the experience of users who may already be dealing with cognitive or sensory challenges.
