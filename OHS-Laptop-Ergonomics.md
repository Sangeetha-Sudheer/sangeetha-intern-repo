# Occupational Health & Safety for Desk-Based Work

## My Current Desk Setup

I currently use a laptop for most of my desk-based work.

In my current setup, the laptop is placed directly on the desk. Because the laptop screen is relatively low, I may need to look downward while working for long periods.

Using the built-in keyboard also means that the screen and keyboard cannot be positioned independently.

## Risks I Identified

The main ergonomic risks in my current setup are:

- neck strain from looking down at the laptop screen;
- shoulder and upper-back discomfort from poor posture;
- wrist discomfort from using the laptop keyboard for long periods;
- eye strain from spending long periods looking at the screen;
- stiffness from sitting in the same position for too long.

## Equipment That Could Improve My Setup

Some equipment that could improve my workspace includes:

- a laptop stand to raise the screen closer to eye level;
- an external keyboard so I can type comfortably while the laptop is raised;
- an external mouse;
- a supportive chair with better back support;
- an external monitor for longer work sessions.

## Workspace Change I Made

I adjusted my laptop position so that I could sit more upright and reduce the amount of time I spent looking downward at the screen.

I also became more aware of my sitting posture and made an effort to keep my back straighter and my shoulders relaxed while working.

This was a simple change, but it made me more aware of how my desk setup can affect my comfort during longer work sessions.

## Habit I Tried

I tried taking regular short breaks from the laptop and checking my posture during the day.

During these breaks, I stood up, stretched, changed position, and gave my eyes a short break from the screen.

I found that this helped me notice when I had been sitting in the same position for too long and encouraged me to move more regularly.

I also tried to remind myself to sit upright and avoid leaning too close to the laptop while working.

## Behavioural Changes

To reduce strain during desk-based work, I can:

- sit upright with my back supported;
- keep my shoulders relaxed;
- keep my wrists in a neutral position while typing;
- take regular movement breaks;
- stand up and stretch during longer work sessions;
- regularly check my posture;
- look away from the screen periodically to reduce eye strain.

## Break and Posture Reminders

I can use Focus Bear to remind myself to take movement and posture breaks during the workday.

A reminder can prompt me to stand up, stretch, walk briefly, or check my sitting position instead of working continuously without a break.

I tried taking regular short breaks from the laptop and checking my posture during the day.

This helped me become more aware of how long I had been sitting and encouraged me to change position instead of remaining in the same posture for a long time.


## Reflection

### What equipment changes can I make to improve my workspace?

The most useful improvement would be raising my laptop screen with a laptop stand and using an external keyboard and mouse.

This would allow the screen to be positioned higher while keeping my hands at a comfortable typing height.

### What behavioural changes can I make to improve posture and reduce strain?

I can improve my posture by sitting upright, keeping my shoulders relaxed, and avoiding leaning toward the laptop screen.

I can also take regular movement breaks and stretch during longer work sessions.

### How can I remind myself to maintain good posture and take breaks?

I can use Focus Bear reminders to schedule movement and posture breaks throughout the day.

Regular reminders would help me avoid becoming too focused on work and forgetting to move or check my posture.
